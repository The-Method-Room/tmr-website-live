How to Choose a Reformer Pilates Studio in South London

November 20, 2026 4 min read By The Method Room

In short: Look for small class sizes, recognised instructor qualifications, clear class levels, transparent pricing and cancellation policies, and an atmosphere that suits you. Try an introductory offer before committing. Practical details like transport and parking matter as much as the equipment.

Reformer Pilates studios are opening across London at a remarkable pace, and ClassPass named Pilates its most-booked class of 2025. That is wonderful for choice, but it can make picking the right studio feel overwhelming. Here is a practical checklist of what to look at before you commit, whether you live in Thornton Heath, Croydon, Mitcham, Norbury, Streatham or beyond.

1. Class size

Group size has a huge influence on your experience. In a large class, an instructor cannot see everyone's form, and it is easy for technique errors to go unnoticed. Look for studios that cap class numbers and are open about it. At The Method Room, classes are limited to a maximum of eight reformers.

2. Instructor qualifications

Pilates instructors are not statutorily regulated in the UK, so it is worth asking about training. Look for instructors with recognised comprehensive qualifications and ongoing professional development, and check whether they are trained to teach clients who are pre or postnatal or returning from injury. Good studios list their instructors and credentials openly.

3. Clear levels and a beginner pathway

A well-run studio will have clearly defined classes for beginners and for progression. Check that there is a beginner class that actually teaches setup, breath and basics, rather than throwing you into a fast-paced session.

4. Pricing and policies

Compare the cost per class across drop-in, class packs and memberships, and look for an introductory offer so you can try before you commit. Read the cancellation and late-arrival policies too, as they vary widely. At The Method Room, new clients can try two classes for £30, and our cancellation policy requires 24 hours' notice.

5. Atmosphere and who it is for

Visit the studio's website and social media, and pay attention to the tone. Is it calm and welcoming? A women-only space can make a real difference if you prefer to exercise in an environment where you feel comfortable, free from distraction and not on show.

6. Booking and communication

Easy online booking, a waitlist and clear communication make it far simpler to build a regular habit. Check how booking works, and how quickly a studio replies to questions.

7. Location and practicalities

The best studio is one you will actually get to. Consider transport links, parking and how long the journey takes. We are at 1050 to 1052 London Road, Thornton Heath CR7 7ND, easily reached by public transport, with free parking nearby, and we welcome women from Croydon, Mitcham, Norbury, Streatham, South Norwood, Crystal Palace and Tooting.

8. Try a class and trust your instincts

The truest test is stepping into the room. Do you feel welcomed? Does the instructor take time to ask about your body? Does the class feel challenging but safe? An introductory class will tell you more than any website can.

Red flags to be aware of

  • Large classes with little hands-on or verbal guidance
  • No information about instructors or their qualifications
  • Unclear pricing or hard-to-find cancellation policies
  • Pressure to commit to a long-term membership before you have tried a class
